This is the table-first working draft. It is meant to be scanned, reordered, and challenged before more prose is added. The full brief remains the research record.

How to read the literature notes. Direct Same question and a similar test. Close Same question or method, but a materially different test. Adjacent Supports the idea without testing this comparison. Each comparison separates what prior work did, what it found, what it called for, and what this project adds. “No direct match found” means none appeared in the primary research reviewed through August 24, 2026. Takeaway. Benchmarks usually record correctness without asking how much confidence the model placed behind the answer. They therefore cannot distinguish a careful belief from a bluff.
Direct precedent Benchmark incentives and abstention
What prior work did
Kalai et al. analyzed why binary accuracy grading rewards guessing. AbstentionBench tested 20 models on 20 datasets containing unanswerable or underspecified questions.
What it found
Accuracy-first evaluation can make a guess score better than admitting uncertainty. AbstentionBench found that abstention remains unreliable and that reasoning fine-tuning made it 24% worse on average.
What it called for
Change the main benchmark incentives, not merely add another hallucination test. Treat uncertainty and justified abstention as scored behavior.
What this project adds
Breadth plus observed behavior. It audits 45 scoring records and 1,088,601 published answers, then uses one scoring rule in prompting and matched training experiments. The incentive diagnosis itself is not new.

Source. EvalEval “Every Eval Ever” datastore, 1,319 per-sample files, pulled 2026-08-15.

Direct precedent Prompting for more realistic confidence
What prior work did
Tian et al. elicited verbal confidence from ChatGPT, GPT-4, and Claude on three factual benchmarks. Xiong et al. compared prompts, sampling, and aggregation across five models and five dataset types. I-CALM announced answer-or-abstain payoffs and added guidance about truthfulness and responsibility.
What it found
Verbal confidence can be more accurate than token probabilities, sometimes cutting calibration error by about half. No elicitation method worked consistently everywhere. Announced payoffs improved selective answering, but the best treatment bundled several instructions.
What it called for
Better ways to elicit uncertainty at test time and turn it into practical deferral or abstention, especially on harder professional tasks.
What this project adds
A matched magnitude estimate. On identical questions across 5 current models and 4 subjects, the rule-and-threshold prompt reduced the confidence gap from 31.3 to 27.0 points. It closed 14% and left 86% in place. Because the prompt bundled the rule and threshold, it cannot identify which wording caused the change.

Source. expanded deterministic prompt test, 120 to 198 items per condition, five models from four developers, eight prompts, pulled 2026-08-22.

The direct test supports the simpler prompt, but not decisively. Confidence prompting plus code produced 1.7 fewer wrong answers and 2.0 more decision-value points per 100 questions. Direct precedent Training models to express uncertainty
What prior work did
RLCR added a Brier-score term to correctness reward. R-Tuning trained models to refuse questions outside their knowledge. Other work has used calibration-aware reinforcement learning across Llama and Mistral models.
What it found
Calibration can improve without sacrificing accuracy, including on some new domains. Learned refusal can also transfer. Recent work warns that poorly designed confidence rewards can be gamed and that the best accuracy-calibration tradeoff depends on the task.
What it called for
Tests across more model families and tasks, broader metacognitive skills, and reward functions that cannot be improved by deliberately giving worse answers.
What this project adds
A cleaner causal contrast. The same starting checkpoint, items, and random seed receive either accuracy-only reward or a strictly proper confidence reward. A fixed answering cost is tested separately. This isolates the training objective more tightly than comparisons between unrelated models.

Direct precedent Does calibration transfer across subjects?
What prior work did
Steyvers et al. fine-tuned two models on general knowledge, math, and open-ended trivia, then tested medical, legal, and truthfulness questions. RLCR also included out-of-domain evaluation.
What it found
Uncertainty communication transfers, but usually less than it improves within the training domain. Transfer between different metacognitive formats is weaker. Multitask training gives broader gains.
What it called for
More model families, more metacognitive tasks, and designs that distinguish broad uncertainty skills from learning one subject or response format.
What this project adds
An explicit same-subject versus outside-subject comparison. Seven independent Qwen pairs were trained on one subject and tested on that subject plus three others. The outside-subject reduction in highly confident errors was 98% as large as the same-subject reduction. Final outside-subject calibration was still worse, so the result shows transfer of the correction, not equal mastery.

Close precedent Replication across model families
What prior work did
RLCR and reward-calibrated RLHF each trained more than one model family.
What it found
Calibration-aware rewards can improve uncertainty behavior in more than one architecture.
What it called for
Broader family coverage and replication under matched training designs.
What this project adds
A pooled replication in GPT-OSS. It repeats the broad transfer pattern seen in Qwen. It does not yet repeat the directional subject-to-subject experiment, so the strongest transfer claim still rests on one family.

Adjacent precedent From calibrated confidence to aligned behavior
What prior work did
The Off-Switch Game models uncertainty about an agent's objective. KnowNo turns calibrated uncertainty into asking for help in robot planning. Other work tests confidence and evidence updating or defines pluralistic alignment.
What it found
Objective uncertainty can create incentives to accept human correction in a formal model. Calibrated runtime uncertainty can improve deferral. Separately, models can be both stubborn about initial answers and too sensitive to criticism. None of these findings shows that factual confidence training creates broad alignment.
What it called for
Systems that preserve uncertainty about objectives, ask for help when needed, update rationally on evidence, and represent multiple human values through multi-objective or pluralistic evaluations.
What this project adds
A matched test of several links in the proposed chain. Confidence training sharply reduced overconfident bad decisions, but did not improve evidence updating or goal revision and barely changed hidden shortcuts. No direct matched study of this full chain was found in this review through August 24, 2026.
What it still cannot show
The shortcut scenarios are short and transparent. They are not evidence of scheming. SHADE-Arena is a stronger model: long tasks, tools, hidden harmful objectives, and monitoring.

Close precedent Evaluation setup and output-format reliability
What prior work did
HELM evaluated 30 models on 42 standardized scenarios and published raw prompts and completions. Alzahrani et al. perturbed answer order and scoring. JSONSchemaBench tested six constrained-generation systems on 10,000 schemas.
What it found
Evaluation details can change results materially: small benchmark perturbations moved rankings by as many as eight places. Format compliance, schema coverage, speed, and answer quality are distinct properties.
What it called for
Standardized conditions, published prompts and completions, robust scoring, and explicit measurement of structured-output reliability.
What this project adds
A prospective admission rule for causal training evidence. A family enters paid reward training only after passing two complete 1,920-response format profiles. A failure means the training effect was not measured. It is never counted as evidence that calibration training failed.

Decision rule. More factual benchmark volume is useful only when it adds a new family or unseen domain. Most new research effort should now go toward hidden, longer-horizon behavior and the causal links between calibrated confidence and broader restraint.
Available benchmarks The harder alignment tests already exist
What prior work did
SHADE-Arena pairs benign tasks with hidden sabotage goals. AgentMisalignment tests shutdown resistance, sandbagging, oversight avoidance, and power seeking. AgentHarm contains 110 malicious multi-step tool tasks. MACHIAVELLI spans 134 games and more than 500,000 social scenarios.
What it found
Current models sometimes complete hidden harmful goals, comply with malicious agent requests, or trade ethical behavior for reward. The behavior depends on capability, monitoring, hidden scratchpads, and even persona prompts.
What it called for
Longer, realistic environments with hidden opportunities for misconduct, benign control tasks, and monitoring that can distinguish lower harm from simple refusal or incompetence.
What this project should add next
The missing causal combination. Run matched accuracy-trained and confidence-trained checkpoints in the same hidden environments.
