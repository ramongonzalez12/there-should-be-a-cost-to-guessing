## about | About me &amp; my motivation

I spent years in quantitative finance before moving into healthcare analytics,
and in both the same lesson kept surfacing: what you choose to measure ends up
being what you get, whatever you intended. A metric with no penalty for
overconfidence produces confident people.

I don't think AI benchmarks are badly designed by carelessness. I think a
leaderboard needs a single number to sort on, and the easiest single number to
justify is the fraction correct, which quietly sets the price of being wrong to
zero. That price should be visible and arguable, not invisible and fixed.
